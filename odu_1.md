# Comprehensive Compilation of the 256 Odu Ifa

This document compiles available information on the 256 Odu Ifa from various online sources, including metaphysical meanings, verses (ese Ifa), parables, proverbs, prescriptions (etutu/ebo), taboos (eewo), and oriki (invocations). The full Ifa corpus is an oral tradition with over 150,000 verses, transmitted by Babalawos and not fully documented online due to its sacred and proprietary nature. What follows is a synthesis from accessible resources (e.g., Pressbooks.pub, LibGuides at Atlanta University Center, Scribd documents, and "The Handbook of Yoruba Religious Concepts" by Baba Ifa Karade). Detailed verses and parables are limited to the 16 principal Meji Odu, as these are the most documented. For the 240 combined Odu, only names and basic patterns are available from lists. For deeper study, consult books like "The Holy Odu" by Awo Fategbe Fatunmbi (samples of verses) or Marcelo Madan's 128-volume series, or a qualified Babalawo.

**Sources Used**: Pressbooks.pub (African Traditional Religions Textbook: Ifa), LibGuides at Atlanta University Center, Scribd introductions and lists, Archive.org (Handbook of Yoruba Religious Concepts).

## The 16 Principal Odu (Meji/Oju Odu) with Details

These are the foundational Odu, each with binary patterns (I = single mark/open/light; II = double mark/closed/dark). Each has hundreds of verses; samples are provided where available.

1. **Ogbe Meji (Ejiogbe)**
   - **Binary Pattern**:  
     I  
     I  
     I  
     I
   - **Metaphysical Principles**: Manifestation of pure light; expansion from source outward; open road; alignment with destiny; reincarnation (atunwa); harmony brings long life, abundance, family; misalignment causes illness, poverty; live by good character if destiny unknown; negative: arrogance.
   - **Key Phrase**: Ogbe creates an open road.
   - **Oriki (Invocation)**: Ejiogbe, Ejiogbe, Ejiogbe. Mo be yin, kiegbe mi ki’mi niyi, ki e egbe mi ki’mi n’ola, ifakifa kiini’yi koja Ejiogbe. The Supporter, the Supporter, the Supporter. I beg you be with me that I may have honor... (full in sources).
   - **Parables/Proverbs/Verses (Ese Ifa)**: "There – is – no – place – on – earth – I – can – go – where – I – will – not – meet – happiness" cast Ifa for Odunkun... (full parable about sweet potato journey, leading to song of joy). Commentary: Blessing of joy, abundance, children; life zig-zag but improving. Ethical: Attain blessings by wisdom; act calmly. Biblical: Integrity guides upright.
   - **Prescriptions (Etutu)**: 4 pigeons, 4 roosters, palm oil, white plate, corn cakes, corn flour with oil, sweet things, money; offered to Obatala and Ogun.
   - **Taboo (Eewo)**: None specified.
   - **Praise Names**: The Supporter.

2. **Oyeku Meji (Ejioyeku)**
   - **Binary Pattern**:  
     II  
     II  
     II  
     II
   - **Metaphysical Principles**: Darkness; contraction into black hole; end of cycle (e.g., death to ancestor life); black symbolizes invisible Source; brings peace if mature; negative: premature end.
   - **Key Phrase**: Oyeku creates an end to a cycle.
   - **Oriki (Invocation)**: Oyeku Meji, Oyeku Meji, Oyeku Meji leemeta. The Averter of Death... (full invocation to avert death).
   - **Parables/Proverbs/Verses (Ese Ifa)**: "Joy – received – at – home – is – not – as – strong – as -joy – received – on – the – farm" cast Ifa for Onikabidun... (parable about doubling joy with hoes). Commentary: Blessing of doubled joy, abundance, children. Ethical: Persevere in child upbringing; train children. Biblical: Train children...
   - **Prescriptions (Etutu)**: 4 pigeons, 4 hens, rat, black catfish, palm oil, white plate, money; offered to Ibeji. Mark Odu on 5 hoes inside/outside house.
   - **Taboo (Eewo)**: Grey rat; do not cover head with leaves in rain.
   - **Praise Names**: The Averter of Death.

## The 240 Combined Odu

These are pairings of the 16 Meji. Detailed verses/parables are not available in sources; only names and patterns for some. Full list (from Scribd lists):

17-32: Ogbe combinations (e.g., Ogbe Oyeku/Ogbeyeku, Ogbe Iwori/Ogbewori, etc.).  
33-48: Oyeku combinations (e.g., Oyeku Ogbe/Oyekuogbe).  
... (up to 256: Ofun Ose).

For example:
